Privacy policy

Effective 23 September 2026

Currawong collects no data. It has no accounts and no server of its own, so nothing you type reaches us.

What Currawong does not do

  • No analytics, no advertising, and no tracking across apps or websites.
  • No crash reporting service. Currawong includes no third-party code of any kind.
  • No location. Currawong never asks for your device's location. The location in your station details is text you type yourself.

What stays on your device

Currawong keeps everything it needs on your device:

  • Your saved channels, your station details (callsign, name and location), your settings and your push-to-talk accessory setup.
  • Node secrets, passwords and tokens, which are kept in the system Keychain.
  • Diagnostic messages about the audio route, written to the device's own system log. They are not sent anywhere by Currawong.

What goes to the networks you connect to

Currawong is a radio. When you connect somewhere, it sends what that network needs, to that network, and to nobody else:

  • Your voice, while you transmit, to the node or reflector you chose, and from there to whoever is listening. Some destinations are also linked to radio transmitters.
  • Your callsign, which every amateur mode carries with each transmission. Identifying yourself is a legal requirement of an amateur licence.
  • The credentials for that network: a node secret or Web Transceiver token for AllStarLink, or your EchoLink password. EchoLink's directory also receives the name and location in your station details, which it shows to other EchoLink users.

Currawong also fetches public information over HTTPS so you can find somewhere to connect:

  • the M17 reflector list, from m17-project.github.io;
  • details of an AllStarLink node you look up, from stats.allstarlink.org (only the node number is sent);
  • the list of public EchoLink proxies, from www.echolink.org;
  • a Web Transceiver token, from allstarlink.org, when you log in with your allstarlink.org callsign and password. The password is used for that login and not stored.

Each of these services is run by its own organisation under its own privacy terms. Currawong only uses them when you ask it to.

Permissions

  • Microphone: to transmit your voice. Currawong only sends audio while it is transmitting, and shows on screen when it is.
  • Bluetooth: to connect a push-to-talk accessory.
  • Local network (iPhone and iPad): to reach a node on your own network, such as one on a Raspberry Pi.

You can withdraw any of these in your device's Settings, under Privacy & Security.

Removing your data

Because nothing is sent to us, there is nothing for us to delete. Deleting Currawong removes its data from your device. The Keychain can keep stored passwords after an app is deleted. To remove them first, clear each password in Currawong's Settings and use Forget token for a Web Transceiver token.

Children

Currawong collects no data from anyone, including children. See age suitability for what the app lets people do.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the new version will be published here with a new date. Its history is public in the app's source repository. For questions, open an issue on GitHub.
